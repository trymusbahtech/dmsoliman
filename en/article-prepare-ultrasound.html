How should you prepare for an ultrasound?

A practical checklist to help you arrive prepared—and an explanation of why instructions vary from one examination to another.

In this articlePreparationWhat to bringDuring the examinationSources
The key point

There is no single set of instructions for every ultrasound. Follow the directions provided by your clinician or examination center: some scans may require fasting, while others may require a full bladder.

Why do preparation instructions vary?

Preparation depends on the organ being examined and the scanning approach. Some abdominal scans may require a period without food, while certain pelvic scans may require drinking water and avoiding urination before the appointment. Do not automatically reuse instructions from a previous examination.

What should you bring?

  • Your referral or the reason for the examination.
  • Relevant previous reports and images, if available.
  • A short list of medicines and important medical history.
  • Comfortable clothing that allows access to the area being examined.

What happens during the examination?

Gel is usually placed on the skin and the probe is moved over the required area to obtain images. You may be asked to change position or hold your breath briefly. Examination time varies with the scan and the clinical questions.

What happens afterwards?

Report delivery and timing vary between centers. Before leaving, ask how you will receive the report and whether you should return to the referring clinician to discuss the findings and next step.

This article provides general education and does not replace instructions specific to your case.
